## What makes a good trusted channel here

**1. Primarily instructional.** The channel's main output must be videos that teach ONE technique at
a time — "how to hit a bunker shot", "how to belay", "how to do a scissor sweep". I do NOT want
channels whose main output is:

- match footage, tournament coverage, highlights or "best points" compilations
- vlogs, travel, challenge videos, or personality/entertainment content
- gear reviews and unboxings as the primary format
- brand or magazine channels where instruction is incidental to marketing
- generic cross-topic how-to aggregators that cover hundreds of unrelated subjects

A channel that posts an occasional review is fine. A channel that is *mostly* reviews is not.

**2. Alive and reasonably sized.** At least ~2,000 subscribers, and posting within roughly the last
6 months. Several otherwise-excellent channels were rejected in my own pass purely for being dormant
— a great back catalogue is still reachable by open search, it just does not earn a channel slot.

**IMPORTANT EXCEPTION — seasonality.** Northern-hemisphere ski and snowboard channels publish
December–March and go silent all summer. For **Skiing and Snowboarding only**, judge liveness on
whether they posted during the last winter season, not on months-since-last-upload.

**3. One channel, one category.** My database enforces one category per channel. So do not propose a
generalist outdoor or fitness channel for several categories — tell me the single best fit, or skip it.

**4. Teaching credibility.** Coaches, certified instructors, academies, national federations and
established teaching brands are strongly preferred over hobbyists. Where a channel has real coaching
credentials, say so.

## What I need for each suggestion

- **Channel name**
- **Channel URL or @handle** — and the **channel ID** (starts with `UC…`) if you can find it. The ID
  is what I actually store; handles get renamed, IDs do not.
- **Category** (one of the seven)
- **Approximate subscriber count**
- **Roughly when they last posted**
- **Why it qualifies** — one line on what they teach and what evidence you have that instruction is
  their main format. Naming 2–3 representative single-technique video titles is the most useful
  evidence you can give me.
- **Any doubt you have** — flag it rather than padding the list

## How many

Aim for **8–15 solid additions per category**, but do not pad. Categories differ in how much good
instructional content exists, and I would much rather get 6 excellent channels for Skiing than 15
where 9 are edge cases. Say plainly when a category is thin.

## Things that tripped me up — please avoid

- I nearly added several channels on the strength of one great video, without checking the rest of
  the channel. Check the channel's recent uploads, not a single hit.
- Dormant channels look healthy in search results because their old videos still rank. Check the
  last upload date.
- Non-English channels are fine if the instruction is genuinely followable, but say so explicitly.
- Do not infer a channel is dead or foreign from auto-translated video titles — YouTube localises
  title metadata, and the underlying channel is often English-language.

## Output format

Group by category. Table or clean list, one row per channel, with the fields above. Put a one-line
verdict at the top of each category ("strong — 12 good candidates" / "thin — only 4 worth adding").
