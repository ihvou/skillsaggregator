# New category research — which categories to add next

**Date:** 2026-08-19
**Question:** which categories should be added to the existing 13?
**Criteria given:** popularity · individual rather than big team sport · sport *or* neighbouring
activity (meditation cited as an example of the latter).

**Method.** Candidate longlist built from individual sports and training-adjacent activities, then
each candidate tested on three things:

1. **Popularity** — participation data from SFIA, NGF, R&A, ITTF, IFSC, SIA.
2. **Individual** — can one person practise it alone.
3. **Decomposability** — the operational gate. Can it be split into 15–30 sub-skills, each of which
   has 10–30 *dedicated single-topic tutorials*? Tested by probing narrow sub-skills through
   `yt-dlp ytsearch` — including the **tail** sub-skills (the 20th–30th page), because that is what
   determines whether a category reaches useful size rather than stalling at eight pages.

Criterion 3 is what separates the list below. Two categories that look equally popular can differ
completely once you ask whether YouTube teaches them *one thing at a time*.

**No first-party demand signal was available.** `internal_user_category_interests` is internal
moderator config (3 rows per category), and `suggestions` (14,370 rows) is the agent link queue.
Neither reflects visitor demand. Everything below is external evidence.

---

# The gate: tutorial vs guided session

The recurring failure across rounds 1 and 2 was topics that are real in a syllabus but exist on
YouTube only as follow-along content. At **category** level this failure is fatal rather than
merely costly — it would produce a whole section of the site whose links are sessions, not lessons,
which is a different product with different moderation rules.

Every candidate below is graded on this axis explicitly.

---

# Tier 1 — build these

## 1. Golf

**Popularity: exceptional.** 48.1M US participants in 2025 (29.1M on-course + 19M off-course at
ranges, simulators and entertainment venues). 3.3M beginners in 2025, and more than 3M new
on-course golfers every year since 2020. Junior participation up 58% since 2019; 18–34 is now the
largest age segment. Globally ~67M players in R&A-affiliated regions.

**Individual: yes**, completely.

**Decomposability: the best of any candidate.** Every narrow sub-skill probed came back with
multiple dedicated, high-view tutorials, including the equipment and rules tail:

- *Swing:* "How to Swing a Golf Club (The EASY way)" (4.3M), "Golf Swing Basics - Easy Steps For Beginners" (3.7M)
- *Fault fixing:* "5 Easy ways to STOP your Golf Slice (FOREVER)" (4.3M), "How to: STOP slicing the golf ball" (1.5M)
- *Short game:* "The SECRET Chipping Technique - MUST TRY!" (3.1M), "Basic Fundamentals for Chipping | Golf with Aimee" (854k)
- *Bunker:* "This Bunker Shot Technique is SO EASY You'll be Shocked" (1.7M), "Rory McIlroy Teaches You Two Different Bunker Shots" (311k)
- *Putting:* "The LAST Putting Lesson You Will Ever Need" (1.1M), "The 5 KEY SKILLS you need to IMPROVE your PUTTING" (174k)
- *Driver:* "TOP 5 DRIVER GOLF TIPS" (3.6M), "World's #1 Coach Reveals EASIEST Way To Hit Driver Straight" (1.3M)
- *Tail — rules:* "Rules of GOLF : How To PLAY GOLF : Golf Rules For Beginners EXPLAINED" (1.3M), "Golf Etiquette Explained Like You're 10" (133k)
- *Tail — equipment:* "What GOLF CLUBS do beginners need??" (899k), "14 Golf Clubs Explained - What To Use and When?" (537k)

**Verdict: build first.** Largest addressable audience, deepest tutorial ecosystem, a huge annual
beginner intake (the exact audience this site serves), and no seasonality problem in most markets.

**Starter sub-skills (~28):** Golf grip · Setup and posture · Golf swing basics · How to hit a driver ·
Iron shots · Fairway woods · Hybrids · Chipping · Pitching · Bunker shots · Putting stroke ·
Reading greens · How to stop slicing · How to stop hooking · Fat and thin shots · Wedge distance
control · Uneven lies · Punch shot · Draw and fade · Course management · Golf rules for beginners ·
Golf etiquette · How to choose golf clubs · Driving range practice routine · Golf warm up ·
Mental game · Getting club fitted · Playing in wind

## 2. Table tennis

**Popularity: the largest raw participation of any candidate.** ITTF estimates 300–350M regular
players worldwide, professional and amateur. 15–16M in the US, stable for years. Low equipment
cost and low access barrier — no club membership, course fee or lift pass.

**Individual: yes** (and solo practice via robot/multiball is a genuine sub-topic).

**Decomposability: strong, and the tail holds.**

- *Core strokes:* "How to do the basic forehand topspin technique?" (223k), "The Perfect Forehand: 4 Details 90% of Players Miss" (290k)
- *Advanced stroke:* "Ultimate BACKHAND FLICK tutorial (basic to advanced)" (75k), "The Secret to a Killer Backhand Flick: It's All About the 'Pause'" (185k)
- *Serve:* "3 KILLER Serves for ALL Players" (1.06M), "Best Table Tennis Serve Tips: Master Topspin, Backspin, and Sidespin!" (342k)
- *Footwork:* "3 KEY TIPS to improve your FOOTWORK in Table Tennis" (155k), "Stop Reaching With Your Arm! The 3 Essential Footwork Patterns" (41k)
- *Tail — defence:* "How to Defend in Table Tennis" (2.7M), "How to do a FOREHAND CHOP - tips from a defensive expert" (89k)
- *Tail — equipment:* "How to choose forehand rubber? (Part 1)" (151k), "How to choose a Table Tennis Racket ? (5 STEPS)" (149k)

**Verdict: build.** Biggest global audience of the shortlist, cheapest to take up, and structurally
the closest match to categories that already work here (Badminton, Tennis, Padel).

**Starter sub-skills (~22):** Shakehand grip · Penhold grip · Ready stance · Forehand drive ·
Backhand drive · Forehand topspin · Backhand topspin · Backhand flick · Forehand push ·
Backhand push · Block · Chop defence · Looping against backspin · Smash · Topspin serve ·
Backspin serve · Sidespin serve · Serve return · Reading spin · Footwork · Choosing a blade and
rubber · Doubles

## 3. Climbing and bouldering

**Popularity: strongest growth trajectory of any candidate.** IFSC puts the global climbing
community above 35M participants; industry estimates reach ~44M regular participants. Participation
grew ~22% annually 2020–2024 against 3–5% for fitness overall. The climbing gym market goes from
$3.2B (2025) to a projected $7.6B (2034) at 9.9% CAGR; 53 new North American gyms opened in 2025.

**Individual: yes** for bouldering; roped climbing needs a partner but the *skills* are taught
individually.

**Decomposability: strong, and unusually well-suited** — climbing tutorials are natively
single-technique.

- *Entry:* "TOP 10 Tips for Beginner Boulderers" (2.9M), "Just Started Climbing? Watch This - Indoor Climbing for Beginners" (480k)
- *Technique:* "How to Flag - A Climbing Technique for Achieving Balance" (376k), "TOP 4 TIPs for Beginner heel hooks - Louis Parkinson" (91k)
- *Grip:* "Crimp Grip vs. Open Grip - MUST WATCH for Beginners" (126k), "Crimp Harder Without Training: Advanced Techniques!" (138k)
- *Safety:* "Rock Climbing: How to Belay" (1.0M), "Become a Belayer I can Trust | Ep.1" (603k)
- *Tail — lead:* "Clipping Techniques for Sport Lead Climbing" (90k), "How to Clip on Lead for Indoor Climbing" (191k), "How to Clip Quickdraws" (196k)

**Verdict: build.** Indoor climbing is year-round, urban, and growing faster than anything else on
this list. Its audience overlaps yours (gym, mobility, strength) without cannibalising it.

**Starter sub-skills (~24):** Climbing shoes and gear · How to tie in (figure 8) · Belaying ·
Lead belaying · Clipping quickdraws · Falling safely · Bouldering for beginners · Climbing footwork ·
Flagging · Heel hook · Toe hook · Crimp grip · Open hand grip · Drop knee · Dyno · Mantle ·
Slab climbing · Overhangs and body tension · Resting on the wall · Reading routes · Climbing grades
explained · Hangboard training · Climbing warm up · Down climbing

# Tier 2 — strong, with a caveat to decide on

## 4. Pickleball

**Popularity: the fastest-growing sport in the US, four years running.** 24.3M American players in
2025, +22.8% year over year and **+171.8% over three years**.

**Individual: partly** — doubles-dominant, but so is Padel, which already works here.

**Decomposability: dense, and the tail holds unusually well.**

- "How to Hit a 3rd Shot Drop in Pickleball" (1.4M), "How To Hit 3rd Shot Drops in Pickleball (RESULTS GUARANTEED)" (540k)
- "How to SPIN your Serve in Pickleball (LEGALLY)" (930k), "How to Serve A Pickleball | Beginner's Guide" (989k)
- "NEVER Lose Another Dink Battle With These 8 Tips" (158k), "How To Dink In Pickleball, The RIGHT Way" (107k)
- *Tail:* "Mastering The RESET in 3 Simple Steps" (122k), "Master the Erne: A MUST HAVE WEAPON in Your Pickleball Game" (126k)

**Caveat to decide on.** It is structurally a third sibling of Padel and Tennis — same page shapes,
same collection patterns. That is an argument *for* it (you have proven machinery) and *against* it
(catalogue starts to look repetitive, and the audience is heavily US-centric compared with the rest
of your catalogue). If you want US traffic growth, this is the single highest-slope option available.

## 5. Skiing and Snowboarding — two categories, not one

**Popularity: solid.** 11.6M active US participants and 61.5M skier visits in 2024–25, the
second-highest on record. Broader winter-sports participation totals ~30M.

**Individual: yes.**

**Decomposability: strong for both.**

- *Ski:* "How to Ski | 7 Steps to Parallel Turns" (2.4M), "How to Make Parallel Turns—Tips for Improving Your Skiing || REI" (919k), "How to Ski Bumps - The Mogul Tutorial" (262k), "Why You Suck At Skiing Moguls" (484k)
- *Snowboard:* "How To Snowboard - 8 STEPS TO CARVING" (727k), "5 Moves To Improve Your Carving Snowboard Turns" (356k), "10 First Butter Snowboard Tricks to Learn" (401k), "5 Fundamental Snowboard Butters | Beginners Guide" (113k)

**Why two categories and not one.** Checked directly by inspecting which channels serve each query:

- *Ski beginner:* Stomp It Tutorials (6.1M), REI (3.4M), Alpine Tutorials with George (307k, 161k)
- *Snowboard beginner:* SnowboardProCamp (2.3M, 2.1M), REI (2.7M), Johnathan Buckhouse (438k)

The instructional ecosystems are **disjoint**. The only channel appearing in both is REI, a generalist
retailer. Every dedicated teaching channel is stance-specific. The technique vocabulary is disjoint
too — parallel turns, moguls and pole plants have no snowboard equivalent; carving on a board,
butters and heelside/toeside have no ski equivalent.

Even the topics that look shared mostly split by sport. Probing "how to use a ski lift" returned SKNG
Ski School and ColoradoSki alongside *"SnowboardProCamp >> How To Ride A Button Lift - Beginner
Snowboard Tutorial"* — the same task, taught separately per sport, because a button lift behaves
completely differently under one edge than under two skis.

Genuinely shared topics are few and peripheral: avalanche safety (evo, ORTOVOX SAFETY ACADEMY,
Salomon TV — none stance-specific), layering and gear, resort etiquette, reading a trail map.
That is roughly 4–5 shared pages against ~20 sport-specific ones each. Merging would produce a
~40-page category with two disjoint halves, diluting both.

Precedent in the existing taxonomy points the same way: Gym (men) and Gym (women) are already split
despite sharing *far* more content than skiing and snowboarding do.

**Caveat to decide on: hard seasonality.** Northern-hemisphere traffic collapses roughly May–October.
Excellent Q4–Q1 acquisition, poor steady-state contribution. Worth building *before* a winter, not
in the middle of the year — which, given today's date, argues for starting collection in September
so pages are populated by December.

## 6. Brazilian jiu-jitsu — best content, smallest audience

Promoted here from Tier 3 after proper testing. My first probe used a bad query and made supply look
thin; it is not.

**Popularity: the weak point, and the estimates conflict badly.** There is no authoritative registry,
and published figures range from ~3M to ~12M practitioners worldwide. The best-anchored derivation is
gym-based: **over 10,000 BJJ gyms in the US averaging 50–100 active members, i.e. 500k–1M US
practitioners**. IBJJF sanctioned events drew more than 18,000 competitors in 2025, up ~12% year over
year, and US interest has roughly doubled in ten years. The market is put at $500M in 2025 growing at
~15% CAGR.

For scale: that is **one to two orders of magnitude smaller** than Tier 1. Golf has 48.1M US
participants; table tennis 300–350M worldwide; climbing 35–44M; pickleball 24.3M US.

**Individual: yes as a learner** — no team, individual progression and ranking.

**Decomposability: outstanding — arguably second only to golf.** BJJ is the most granularly taught
sport tested. Every position and submission probed returned dedicated, high-view instruction:

- *Escapes:* "How To Do The Perfect BJJ Side Control Escape by John Danaher" (1.03M), "The First 3 Mount Escapes You Need To Know in BJJ" (742k), "How To Do The Perfect BJJ Mount Escape by John Danaher" (697k)
- *Submissions:* "How to Do Kimura | MMA Fighting" (1.4M), "Kimura From Closed Guard For White Belts" (807k), "BJJ Moves: Arm Bar From Guard by John Danaher" (705k), "How To Perform The Perfect Rear Naked Choke by John Danaher" (445k)
- *Positions:* "How To Build The Perfect Half Guard Game by John Danaher" (661k), "Easy to Use Entire Closed Guard System" (264k)
- *Passing:* "5 Tips To Pass ANY Guard by John Danaher" (1.13M), "The MOST Important Guard Passing Principle" (242k)
- *Takedowns:* "How to Do a Double Leg Takedown for MMA & BJJ" (670k), "Basic Double Leg Takedown" (352k)
- *Entry:* "Starting Jiu Jitsu? What to Know Before Your 1st Class" (1.4M), "WHITE BELT POSITIONAL GUIDE" (335k)

It supports 30–40 sub-skills comfortably — more than any Tier 1 candidate.

**Engagement is unusually high per participant.** Watching instructionals between sessions is core to
BJJ culture in a way it is not for most sports, so raw participation understates the addressable
video-learning audience. That partly offsets the size gap — but only partly.

**The three real caveats:**

1. **Partner-dependent.** Earlier framing overstated this as a content problem; it is not. The use
   case — learn the detail, drill it at the gym that evening — is a strong fit. But it does mean the
   audience is *people who already train*, a committed funnel rather than a beginner-acquisition one.
   Golf brings 3.3M new beginners a year; BJJ brings people who already pay a gym membership.
2. **The ecosystem's centre of gravity is intermediate-to-advanced.** Danaher, Gordon Ryan, Craig
   Jones and Lachlan Giles dominate. Beginner content exists and performs, but this is the inverse of
   golf, where beginner content is the centre of gravity.
3. **Gi vs no-gi splits some of the repertoire**, which either duplicates pages or forces a choice.

**Verdict.** A legitimate build on content quality — it would produce one of the best-populated
categories in the catalogue. It is held back purely by audience size. Rank it alongside pickleball as
the fourth build, choosing between them on intent: **pickleball buys reach, BJJ buys engagement.**
If the catalogue is optimising for traffic growth right now, pickleball wins; if for a deep, loyal
niche, BJJ does.

**Do not merge it with Muay Thai into a "Martial arts" category** — the same disjoint-ecosystem
argument that separates skiing from snowboarding applies here, and Boxing already stands alone.

# Tier 3 — viable, smaller, or qualified

**Muay Thai / kickboxing** — strong supply ("5 Common Muay Thai Roundhouse Mistakes" 3.1M,
"Dominate the Muay Thai Clinch with Petchboonchu" 930k, "Building a Strong Teep Step by Step with
Tawanchai" 478k, "Muay Thai Basics: Elbows" 110k). Individual, and shares an audience with your
existing Boxing category, so it cross-links naturally. Smaller than Tier 1 but the cleanest bolt-on.

**Skateboarding** — excellent, natively single-trick supply ("HOW TO KICKFLIP THE EASIEST WAY
TUTORIAL" 8.7M, "How To Ollie In Under 4 Minutes" 2.3M, "HOW TO DROP IN THE EASIEST WAY TUTORIAL!"
1.3M). Trick-based decomposition is a perfect fit for the sub-skill model. *Caveat:* young audience,
weakest commercial profile on this list.

**Archery** — strong supply ("You're Doing It Wrong: How to Shoot an Arrow" 1.1M, "How to shoot a
recurve bow | Archery 360" 850k, "7 ESSENTIAL TIPS FOR STARTING ARCHERY" 787k), very individual,
decomposes cleanly (stance, nock, draw, anchor, release, aiming, tuning, equipment). Smaller
participation than Tier 1–2.

**Hiking / backpacking** — strong supply ("How to Use Trekking Poles (Like a Boss)" 1.2M,
"How to Pack a Backpack || REI" 955k). Hiking and trail running were called out by SFIA as
continuing a decade-long growth trend. *Caveat:* the content is weighted toward gear and logistics
rather than technique, so it is a slightly different page type from the rest of the catalogue.

**Chess** — enormous audience and it decomposes beautifully ("Basic Chess Openings Explained" 4.8M,
"How To Learn & Study Chess Openings" 7.7M, "EASY CHESS ENDGAMES: King & Pawns" 1.7M,
"Introduction to Pawn Endgames | GM Naroditsky" 303k). Individual, year-round, zero access barrier.
*Caveat:* it is a mind sport, and the largest brand departure on this list from "sport & training".
Purely a positioning call — the supply and demand are not in doubt.

**Olympic weightlifting** — good supply ("HOW TO SNATCH / A Visual Guide" 832k, "How to Master the
Snatch in Olympic Weightlifting" 797k) but it overlaps Gym (men) heavily. Better handled as
sub-skills there (power clean is already recommended in the round-2 audit) unless you want the full
snatch / clean & jerk / jerk-variant / overhead-squat progression, which would justify a category.

**Indoor rowing** — good supply ("US Olympians Teach Us To Row" 2.3M, "The Official 2026 Rowing Form
Checklist" 1.8M, "Correct Rowing Machine Technique | Concept2" 1.4M) but I estimate only 12–15
genuine sub-skills (catch, drive, finish, recovery, damper, rate, pacing, workouts, common faults).
Thin for a standalone category; sits more naturally as sub-skills inside a cardio context.

**Dance** — viable but it is really three categories. Salsa decomposes best and is well supplied
("Salsa Beginners 1 - Salsa Basic Step for the Absolute Beginner" 4.1M, "7 Salsa Moves for Beginners"
622k). Hip hop skews follow-along ("7 mins beginners hiphop dance class warm up... follow along"
2.7M) though real vocabulary tutorials exist ("60 Hip Hop Dance Steps | Hip Hop Dance
Vocabulary/Foundation" 63k).

**Kayaking / stand-up paddleboarding** — modest supply and a thin tail ("Efficient Sea Kayak Forward
Paddling Technique" 204k, but "Step back turn basics - paddle board lesson" 689). Too small.

**Horse riding** — better supply than expected ("How to Post Trot On a Horse" 548k, "POSTING TROT FOR
BEGINNERS (TIPS)" 306k) and it decomposes, but the access barrier is the highest on this list.

# Rejected, with evidence

## Meditation — do not build as a category

This was the example given in the brief, so it got a careful test. **It fails the tutorial gate
comprehensively.** Every sub-skill probed returned *guided sessions*, not instruction:

- "Guided Wim Hof Method Breathing" (116M), "Wim Hof Method Guided Breathing for Beginners" (26M)
- "Guided Loving Kindness Meditation... | Buddhism In English" (2.2M), "10-Minute Loving Kindness Meditation | Guided Metta Practice with Sharon Salzberg" (208k)
- "Compassionate Body Scan - 20 Minute Guided Meditation" (1.9M), "Mindfulness Meditation Body Scan" (2.0M)
- "Box Breathing Exercise | TAKE A DEEP BREATH | Pranayama Series" (2.5M), "5 Minutes Box Breathing Relaxation Exercise" (1.4M)
- Even the noting technique — the most teachable item on the list — returns one instructional video
  ("Headspace | Meditation | The Noting technique" 7.9M) and then a cliff to sub-2k-view guided sits.

A meditation category would be a catalogue of *sessions*, not tutorials. That is a different
product: different moderation criteria, different page shape, and your existing "10–30 curated
tutorials teaching one specific thing" promise would not describe it.

**The teachable slice is already captured.** Yoga → Pranayama (Breath Control) has 33 published
videos and covers ujjayi and box breathing — the part of this space that *is* instructional.

If you still want the wellness audience, the honest options are (a) extend Pranayama with one or two
more breathwork pages inside Yoga, or (b) accept guided sessions as a distinct link type and treat
it as a separate product surface. Neither is "add a category" as currently defined.

## Stretching / mobility — borderline, and it overlaps three existing categories

Real instructional content exists ("Improve Thoracic Spine Mobility (4 Best Exercises)" 621k,
"Top 5 Thoracic Spine Mobility Drills" 649k, "Fix Anterior Pelvic Tilt FAST: A Science Based Guide"
66k), but the bulk is follow-along routines ("Fix 'Anterior Pelvic Tilt' in 10 Minutes/Day (Daily
Exercise Routine)" 9.9M, "The REAL WAY to UNLOCK YOUR HAMSTRINGS (Follow Along Stretch)" 2.1M).
It also overlaps Yoga, Gym (men) → Mobility warm-up and Gym (women) → Mobility and stability. The
round-2 audit already routes the good parts there (splits under Yoga).

# Recommendation

**Build, in this order:**

1. **Golf** — biggest audience, deepest supply, 3M+ new beginners a year, no seasonality.
2. **Table tennis** — largest global participation on the list, lowest access barrier, closest fit
   to machinery you already run.
3. **Climbing and bouldering** — fastest structural growth, year-round and indoor, adjacent to your
   existing gym audience.

**Then decide on one of:**

- **Pickleball** if the goal is maximum US growth slope — accepting that it is a third racket-sport
  sibling.
- **Brazilian jiu-jitsu** if the goal is depth and engagement rather than reach — best
  decomposability on the list after golf, but a 500k–1M US audience against golf's 48.1M.
- **Skiing + Snowboarding** as **two separate categories** if you want a seasonal acquisition push —
  start collection in September so pages are full before December.

**Cheapest bolt-on:** Muay Thai, which shares the Boxing audience and cross-links to it.

**Do not build:** Meditation, as a category. The demand is real; the content type is not.
